Build the equipment ledger for a site store where the same drill can never be in two hands.

A tool store on a construction site. Sixty-odd assets, a few dozen workers, and a paper book that nobody trusts. Replace the book. The hard part is not the form — it is that the ledger has to still be right after two people click at once, after somebody logs a return an hour late, and after you are asked who held the gas detector at 14:20 last Tuesday.

01

What you're building

The brief

A store keeper stands at a hatch with a laptop. Workers come to the hatch to take equipment out and bring it back. Four things the system must be able to do:

01
Issue

Hand an asset to a worker — now, or against a reservation they made earlier.

02
Return

Take it back. Possibly from a different worker, possibly damaged and going out of service.

03
Reserve

Claim an asset for a future window. Two overlapping claims on one asset must be impossible.

04
Reconstruct

The whole store as it stood at any past instant, and the full life of any one asset.

Everything else is yours. How many pages there are, what the API looks like, how the collections are shaped, whether the ledger is events or rows, where validation lives, what renders on the server — none of that is specified here.

Scope discipline

No authentication, no roles and permissions, no email, no file uploads, no barcode scanning, no mobile app, no multi-site. The store keeper is identified by picking a name from a list, and the worker the same way. We would rather see the ledger provably correct than six screens done thinly.

02

Domain

The things in it

Asset
A single physical item with an identity — HARN-014, not "a harness". It has a kind, and it may require a certification to hold. It can be taken out of service and later brought back.
Worker
A person who can hold assets. Holds zero or more certifications, each with an expiry date.
Reservation
A claim on one asset for a window of time in the future, made by a worker.
Movement
A recorded fact: this asset went to this worker at this time, or came back at this time. Movements are what the ledger is made of.

That is the vocabulary, not the schema. Whether a movement is one document or two, whether an asset carries a denormalised current holder, whether reservations and movements live together — your call.

03

Constraints

The rules you have to work inside

One holder, everAn asset is held by at most one worker at any instant. Not "usually" — never, including when two requests arrive in the same millisecond.
Reservations don't overlapTwo reservations on the same asset may not share a minute. Adjacent is fine, overlapping is not.
Certification gates issueSome assets require a certification. Expired on the day of issue means refused, with a reason a human can read.
Out of service is realAn unserviceable asset cannot be issued or newly reserved. Reservations already standing against it are your problem to resolve — decide, and say what you decided.
Late entries happenThe keeper logs a 09:00 return at 11:40. The ledger records both when it happened and when it was written, and they are not the same field.
Corrections, not erasuresA wrongly recorded movement can be fixed. The history must show that a correction was made, not quietly read as though the mistake never happened.
Nothing lands twiceA double-clicked button, a retried request, a refreshed tab mid-submit — one movement, however many times it arrived.
Any instant, answerable"Who held what at 14:20 last Tuesday" is a supported question, answered from the ledger, not from a cache you happened to keep.

One holder, corrections, nothing twice, and any instant are not negotiable. Everything the system does has to hold them.

On concurrency

Two issue requests for the same asset can arrive at the same moment, repeatedly. Exactly one must succeed and the other must fail with a sensible answer. "Mongo is fast enough that it won't happen" is not a design — be able to point at the line that makes it impossible, and to say what it costs.

04

Data

What has to be in there before we look

Ship a seed command that gives the store a past — enough that the history view has something to show and the reconstruction question has a real answer.

  • Roughly 60 assets across a handful of kinds, some requiring certification, at least one out of service.
  • Roughly 12 workers, with certifications — including one expired and one expiring inside the seeded window.
  • Thirty days of movements: ordinary issues and returns, a few still outstanding, at least one overdue, at least one late-logged entry, and at least one correction.
  • Some reservations, past and future, including one that was never collected.
Repeatable

Seeding twice must not double the store, and the data should be deterministic — the same command gives the same store, so the scenario in your recording can be run again.

05

Assessment

What we will do to it

We will not read your code first. We will run it, try to break the ledger, and then read the code to find out how you stopped us.

The main scenario

Seed the store. Issue an asset, then try to issue the same one to somebody else — concurrently, twice at once, and again from two browser tabs. Return it with a backdated time. Correct that time. Reserve it for a window that overlaps an existing reservation. Ask what the store looked like an hour before all of this. We expect one holder throughout, a history that shows the correction, and an answer to the "as of" question that is consistent with every screen.

And then

  • Double-click submit; replay the same request twice with the same payload; refresh mid-submit.
  • Issue an asset requiring a certification to a worker whose certification expired yesterday.
  • Take an asset out of service while it is issued, and while it is reserved.
  • Return an asset that isn't out. Return it twice. Return it as a different worker than the one holding it.
  • Backdate a return to before its issue. Backdate a movement into the middle of a later one.
  • Reserve a window in the past. Reserve a window that ends before it starts. Reserve for a whole year.
  • Ask for an "as of" instant that falls exactly on a movement's timestamp, and one before the store existed.
  • Kill the API mid-request and see what the browser is left claiming.
  • Read the ledger straight out of Mongo and check it says the same thing your screens do.
  • Then we will ask you why you built it that way. Bring your reasoning, not your library list.
06

Submission

What to send

Deliverables

  • A Git repository with real commit history — not one "initial commit" dump.
  • A README: how to run both sides, how to seed, how to run the invariant checks, the model you chose and why, how you made concurrent issue impossible, what you'd do with another day, and what you knowingly left out.
  • A screen recording, two or three minutes: issue, a refused issue, a backdated return, a correction, and the "as of" answer.
Note on the scenario

The site store, the assets and the workers are invented for this test. Nothing here is drawn from a live system, and your submission stays your own work — we ask only that you don't publish it as a solved interview exercise while we're still using it.

Questions are welcome. If something here is ambiguous, ask — and if you'd rather make a judgement call and document it in the README, that's an equally good answer. Deciding under ambiguity is part of the job.
